# Converting between games
Bring an outfit from one game or body to another, e.g. Fallout 4 CBBE to Skyrim SE CBBE or UBE: importing it, conversion references, weights, partitions, and how shaders, materials and texture maps differ between the games.

The games differ in NIF format, skeleton, shaders and texture layout. The shape and sliders carry over; bone weights, partitions and materials have to be made again for the target game.

## Importing

`load_project` and `import_file` only take the open project's game. **`import_from_game`** brings in another game's NIF, or a set of another game's BodySlide folder (its `.osp`, with `set`; the shape data comes from that folder's `ShapeData`):

- Shapes come over in global space (skin space and node transforms applied), with UVs, normals and vertex colors.
- From a set, the outfit's own slider data and zaps come along with the slider flags (`sliders: "local"`, the default; `"all"` also takes data from the body's shared files).
- Bone weights don't, since the skeletons differ; the shapes come in skinned without weights.
- `skipShapes` leaves out shapes, e.g. the source game's body when you don't need it.
- `dataFolders` (the mod's or game's Data folder that holds `Materials/`) lets it read the Fallout 4 material (BGSM) and set up the Skyrim shader from it. The result lists each shape with its material file and texture paths, `materialNotes` on what doesn't map (each note once, with its shapes) and `texturesNotInGameData`. `verbose: true` adds every material value; `read_material` reads a BGSM or BGEM on its own.
- The textures stay where the mod has them. `asset_folders` `op: "add"` with the mod's folder makes the window find them (and its material files and archives) without copying them into the game's Data folder; the setting is kept per game.
- The `ImportFromGame` automation step does the same in `run_steps` and scripts (`FilePath`, `Set`, `DataFolders`, `Shapes`, `SkipShapes`, `Sliders`), for converting many files in one call (see `basics`, "Many outfits at once").

**Import the source body too** and make it the reference with `set_reference_shape`. Then the source's clipping can be measured, and the next `load_reference` carries its zaps (e.g. `BodyZap`) onto the target body.

## Workflow

Measured on a Fallout 4 CBBE wetsuit with sleeves, belt and thigh holsters, converted to Skyrim SE CBBE and then UBE:

1. **Import** the outfit and its body with `import_from_game`, and set the body as reference.
2. **Load the conversion reference** for the two bodies. Look for "Convert: …" templates in `list_reference_templates`, e.g. "Convert: FO4 CBBE To SSE CBBE" (also "SK CBBE To SSE CBBE" for Skyrim LE, and from other bodies like "Vanilla To CBBE" or "UNP … To CBBE"). At sliders 0 it should match the source body: `analyze_clipping` close to what step 1's body gave (the wetsuit 60 against 26, the belt 585 against 544).
3. **Conform it** (see `conform-sliders`). The Fallout 4 to Skyrim slider also lowers the arms from Fallout 4's rest pose to Skyrim's, which pushes the forearms through tight sleeves: `auto_fix_clipping` on that slider alone took the sleeves from 301 to 20 new clipping vertices. Fix it again after `analysis_exclusions` `op: "zaps"` with `zaps: []`, so it also holds with the body zap off. Rigidify the slider on buckles, clips and pouches, then bake it: slider at 1, `set_base_shape`.
4. **Load the target body** ("CBBE Body"), conform its sliders, rigidify again.
5. **Weights:** `copy_bone_weights` from the target body (see `bone-weights`). When the source had breast and butt physics, copy from the physics variant ("CBBE Body Physics"). A body without hands leaves the old hand bones empty; `delete_bones` removes them.
6. **Partitions or segments:** `copy_partitions` from the target body (below).
7. **Shader and textures** (below).
8. **Check** as for any conversion (`body-conversion`): sweep, presets, poses, `validate_project`.
9. For another body of the target game, continue from the result as a body conversion, e.g. CBBE SE to UBE through "3BA to UBE 2.0" (see `body-conversion`). With 3BA or UBE, copy weights with `excludeBones` for the genital and anus physics bones, or a suit's crotch follows their physics.

## Outfits made for a body preset

Many mods model their outfits on their own body shape, a preset of the body (e.g. a CBBE preset with bigger breasts and hips), not on the body's base shape. The game's conversion template expects the base shape, so take the preset off first, in an instance of the **source** game:

1. **Find the preset.** Mods often ship it (BodySlide `SliderPresets`); copy it into this BodySlide folder's `SliderPresets`. Load the body as reference, import a few skin-tight pieces, and `analyze_clipping` with `preset` for each candidate and with no sliders. The right preset has the fewest vertices inside the body and many close to it. On a Fallout 4 mod a bodysuit had 2611 vertices inside plain CBBE and 100 inside its "nude" preset.
2. **One preset rarely fits every piece.** Presets of one mod often differ only at the breasts: a "nude" one and an "outfit" one with the breasts pushed up and together. Natural tops, swimsuits and bodysuits fitted the nude preset, while corsets and push-up tops fitted the outfit one (a corset: 865 inside the nude preset, 77 inside the outfit one). Nipple piercings and chains only touched the nipples with one preset. Measure each upper-body piece against each preset and take the best. Pieces below the breasts measure the same with every preset.
   - **Measure the front over the breasts separately.** Counts over the whole piece are dominated by the sides and back, which fit every preset. A tube top fitted a "nude" preset by them (179 inside, 1947 of 2846 within 0.6), but its front stood 1.5 in front of the breast tip, and the conversion keeps that gap: after the bake its breasts looked too big on the plain body. `measure_gap` with a region in front of the breasts (on CBBE y above 4, z −36 to −16) and `against` the UniBoob shape gives the signed gaps there. Use UniBoob with the body's sliders (load the body, `import_file` the UniBoob mesh, `conform_sliders`), so it follows each preset; it measures cloth spanning the cleavage too. A fitting preset has a median of 0.2–0.3 and few vertices more than 0.2 inside or 1 outside, apart from the elastic hems.
   - **If no preset fits, make one.** Start from the closest preset and raise the breast size sliders one at a time, measuring as above. For the tube top, the mod's preset with CBBE `BreastsNewSH` 0.5 took the median from 0.55 to 0.26 and the vertices more than 1 out from 30% to 6%. `save_preset` it, then make its conversion references as below. Check slider names against `list_sliders`: a preset entry for a slider the body doesn't have is silently ignored ("BreastsCleavage" did nothing on Fallout 4 CBBE, whose slider is `BreastCleavage`).
   - **Measure every upper-body piece, not just the ones that look off.** Measure the breast front (|x| < 7) and the breast sides (|x| 7–14) separately, for each candidate preset, as the median gap and the shares more than 1 out and inside: `measure_gap` with both `regions` (the sides with `mirrorX: true`) and the candidates in `presets` (`presetWeights: ["high"]` for a mod's own presets, which have one weight) measures them in one call and ranks them (`ranking`, lowest `score` first; `targetGap` 0.2 by default). Ten pieces of one mod had been baked with the mod's "v2 nude" preset but fitted "v3 nude" or a custom one far better. A bikini's cups went from 0.61 to 0.28 off the breasts, and a dress's breast sides from 0.58 to 0.25. Weigh the gap against clipping: each step of `BreastsNewSH` brought the cloth closer but put more of it inside. A middle preset (0.45 between 0.35 and 0.5) suited pieces that clipped 13–40% at 0.5 and stood 0.7 off at 0.35. Trim on the cloth tells too: a top's metal clipped 86 vertices on the mod's preset and 567 at 0.45, on the source itself.
   - **Variants that are meant to be loose** (a dress in a looser cut) still come from the same body. Bake them with the preset their fitted sibling matches, which keeps their looseness: the wrong preset added 0.5–0.7 on top of it.
3. **Make a conversion reference per preset:** load the body, `apply_preset`, `snapshot_save`, `make_conversion_reference` (e.g. slider `PresetToBase`). Check the direction: at 0 `analyze_clipping` should give the preset's numbers, at 1 those of the base shape. Delete any outfit shapes you imported to test, then `save_project` it as a reference set with `copyReference: true`. Then load the saved set again and check a landmark with `get_shape_geometry`: at slider 1 the breast tip must sit where it is on the plain body (in Fallout 4 CBBE, vertex 12092 at y 9.23). Clipping counts alone missed a set whose breasts only went part of the way.
4. **Per piece:** `load_reference` with its set, `conform_sliders`, slider at 1, `set_base_shape`, `export_file` the NIF. Then `import_from_game` those NIFs into the target game and convert as above.
5. **Cloth between the breasts** that doesn't follow the cleavage (a shirt or dress top spanning from breast to breast, an open front with a hem across the gap) stays where the bigger breasts pushed it out: the body vertices under it, deep in the cleavage, barely move with the preset, so after the bake the cloth floats in front of the smaller breasts. Make a second conversion reference per preset from the UniBoob shape: its surface spans the gap, so the cloth over it moves in with the breasts.
   - **Make it from the stock UniBoob reference with the preset applied:** load the "CBBE Uniboob (no nipples)" reference with its own sliders, `apply_preset`, `make_conversion_reference`, `save_project` with `copyReference: true`. Its breast sliders match the body's: with two Vtaw presets it stayed on the body's breast fronts, sides and underbust as closely as at rest (within 0.36, the flattened nipples), while its fill bridged the cleavage by up to 0.85, one even surface like a tube top. Load it with `mergeSliders: false, appendNewSliders: true` (a `LoadReference` step's defaults load no sliders, and the preset then does nothing).
   - **Don't conform the UniBoob mesh to the body instead.** A conform gives each fill vertex the move of the nearest body vertex, on the inner breast, so with a preset the fill sank into the cleavage, and every top converted from it bowed or dented there. Smoothing the fill's slider data afterwards (masked to the fill, `smooth_slider_deltas` without a slider) flattened it but left it behind the bigger breasts with folds at its edge; widening the smoothing into the inner breasts cut 1.4 into the body; a wide conform of the fill (`proximityRadius: 15`) let it sink again.

   Many pieces mix both (cups that follow each breast with laces or a bridge across the gap), so blend per vertex instead of choosing per piece (`conform-sliders`, "Blending two conversions"):
   - Conform the UniBoob reference, `clone_slider` it to `ConvUni`, load the body's reference with `mergeSliders: true, appendNewSliders: false` and conform only the conversion slider. Conform both with `SmoothResults: true, SmoothIterations: 12, SmoothStrength: 0.5` (see "Spikes under the breasts" below).
   - Decide by the distance to the **body**, not the UniBoob shell: `mask_ops` `from_analysis`, metric `clipping` against the body reference, `sliderValues` with the conversion slider at 0, `threshold: -0.6`, `target: "fix"`, `grow: 1`, `feather: 3`. Cloth within 0.6 of the body stays unmasked and follows the body. Farther cloth is masked and takes the UniBoob. Inside the UniBoob shell is the wrong test: the shell's front lies ahead of the cleavage, so laces between the cups were inside it (567 of 882 vertices) while still on the body.
   - `clear_slider_data` `ConvUni` with the mask, invert, `clear_slider_data` the conversion slider, clear the mask, both sliders to 1, `set_base_shape`.
   - On a laced top with a pushed-together preset, the body-only bake bunched the inner cup edges outward at the cleavage; the blend laid them flat and the laces straight.
   - Mask and blend have no automation step for the measurement, so batch it as `run_steps` (load, import, conform, clone, second reference, conform, `ClearMask`, `InvertMask`), `mask_ops`, `run_steps` (clear, invert, clear, bake, export). The full mask before `mask_ops` gives the UniBoob conversion to shapes with no vertex near the body (bridges, zips, pistols), which `from_analysis` leaves unchanged. Chain each piece's finishing steps with the next piece's setup to need two calls per piece, and keep a list: running the finishing steps on the wrong piece exports it under the next piece's name.
   - Blended this way, 61 upper-body pieces of a Fallout 4 mod (tops, corsets, jackets, dresses, swimsuits, harnesses, necklaces, nipple chains) came out right. Tight pieces stayed almost entirely on the body conversion. A T-shirt went mostly UniBoob and spanned the chest evenly (11 vertices clipping at rest, max 0.16), and a dress's skirt was unaffected because both references agree away from the chest.
   - **Keep the source game's gaps.** After the Fallout 4 → Skyrim conversion every top sat about 0.1 farther off the breasts than in Fallout 4, evenly everywhere. 0.06 of that is the stock "FO4 CBBE To SSE CBBE" reference, which ends outside the CBBE SE body on the chest; the smoothed conform adds the rest. To take it off, import the reference's own mesh (`ShapeData/References/FO4 CBBE To SSE CBBE.nif`) as an outfit shape and rename it, e.g. `OldBody`. Conform it with the cloth (without smoothing), bake, and load the target body. Then `fit_to_body` the cloth with `gapMode: "fromShape", gapFrom: ["OldBody"]`, and delete `OldBody`. On a bikini top the cups went from 0.37 to 0.31 (0.28 in Fallout 4), the straps from 0.30 to 0.17 (0.19). Leave straps that must stay straight out of the fit; their ends moved less than 0.03. On whole dresses and tops the default fit flipped up to 69 triangles and stretched tiny edges 35-fold. `stiffness: 4, clippingPasses: 0` kept them intact (0–14 flips) and every region of a dress within 0.04 of its Fallout 4 gap. Without the clipping passes, cloth the source already had inside the body stays inside. That matches the source, so compare with its clipping on its own body before pushing it out.
   - **Tops that span the cleavage** (tube tops, V-tops) need no blend and no fixing in the middle: conform them to the UniBoob conversion reference alone (made as above), bake, load the body and `fit_to_body` with `gapMode: "keep", minGap: 0.05` to push out what clips, without masks. Three tube tops went from 153–263 clipping vertices to 0, with 2–6 flipped triangles and a mean move of 0.03–0.05, and kept the source's even front. The crease under the breasts still came out a little lumpy in the middle: smooth it like a smooth brush stroke, `mask_ops` `box` from (-7, 0, -34) to (7, 40, -26) on CBBE, `invert`, `smooth` the mask 4 steps, `smooth_shapes` 20 iterations at 0.3, clear the mask. That matched the user's brush fix within 0.06 on average and added no clipping. Mask sleeves (`mask_ops` `bone_weighted` on the arm bones, smoothed 4 steps) out of the push-out: they clip into the arms by 1.5 because of the arm pose, which the push-out can't take (170–235 flipped triangles), and it is a matter for the sleeves on their own.
   - **Don't patch a bowing or sunken middle with box masks.** On a conformed UniBoob reference whose fill had sunk (see above), every patch showed: fitting a box between the breasts onto the UniBoob mesh left a square dent (the middle 0.5–1.9 closer than the source, the inner breasts 0.4 looser), smoothing under the breasts in a box left an edge, and keeping each vertex's source gap with `fit_to_body` `gapFrom` the preset body let the cloth sag into the smaller cleavage. Fix the reference instead.
   - **Don't blend for the target body's sliders.** The blend is for one big preset change. For the target game's body sliders, conform the upper-body pieces to the stock UniBoob reference alone (in Skyrim "CBBE Uniboob": it lies on the body everywhere except the cleavage fill, median 0.00 at rest). Blended by the same distance mask, the cloth creased across the cleavage and the underbust with CBBE Curvy, where the body-following and the UniBoob-following vertices met. UniBoob alone removed the crease: on 13 tops the folds of the breast sliders fell by up to half (V-Top V3 130 → 62, Top 3 147 → 60), and clipping with the presets stayed the same or fell. Conform the sliders UniBoob has no data for from the body, as with chains.
6. **Trim on top of cloth** (zippers, metal edging, laces, nets, straps, holstered pistols) gets its own mask and smoothing, so it drifts off the cloth under it: a zipper along a V neckline floated off and sank into the cloth below the breasts. After the blend, conform the trim to the converted cloth instead:
   - set both conversion sliders to 1, `NewCombinedSlider` `Final`
   - `SetReferenceShape` the cloth, `ConformSliders` only the trim shapes (`TargetRegex` with e.g. `^(metal|laces)$`) with `SliderNames: ["Final"]`
   - `DeleteShape` the old body (now an outfit shape), both conversion sliders to 0 and `Final` to 1, `SetBaseShape`, `ExportFile` with `WithRef: true` so the cloth, now the reference, is written too.
   Placeholders work inside `TargetMeshes` entries, so one step list serves every piece. To find the pieces that need it, compare each shape's distance to the other shapes in the source and in the bake (nearest vertex, for vertices within 1 unit in the source): laces drifted 0.43 → 0.03, a top's metal 0.68 → 0.32, a net 0.4 → 0.17. Rigid parts hanging from a strap (pistols in holsters) still drift about 0.6 relative to each other; `rigidify_slider` them in the quality pass.
   - **Metal trim with small and long parts** (rings and studs on wires, zipper teeth): after conforming the trim to the cloth, `rigidify_slider` `Final` on it with `clearance: -1`, with `maxPartLength: 5` so the parts longer than about 5 units keep bending (`bendingParts` in the result counts them). Rigidifying the long wires too pulled the metal up to 1.95 off the cloth. Without rigidify, small rings bent by up to 0.83. Done this way, the small parts kept their shape (worst 0.02) and the metal stayed on the cloth (worst 0.27).
   - Leave out the underbust smoothing (item 9) for pieces with rigid trim: the trim follows the smoothed cloth and the rigid parts bend again.
   - In the target game, `fit_to_body` such trim with `rigidParts: true` and `maxPartVertices` below the long parts' size, so the rings move whole while the wires bend. Gems set into a dress went in rigidly the same way, with no flipped triangles.
7. **Spikes under the breasts:** a plain conform of a big preset change leaves jagged cloth where it bridges the crease under the breasts. Each cloth vertex takes its move from its nearest body points, and those switch between the underside of the breast, which moves a lot, and the ribcage, which barely moves. This isn't caused by blending: body-only, UniBoob-only and blended bakes had the same spikes (tank top 79/79/81 vertices, Top 2 196/190/189). Smoothing the conform result fixes it: 12 iterations at 0.5 took both pieces to 0, while clipping at rest stayed the same (115 against 111 vertices, max 0.14) and the mean gap change rose only from 0.01 to 0.03. 3 or 6 iterations left some spikes. To find them across a batch, export the bakes and the sources to OBJ and compare each vertex's distance from its neighbours' average: vertices where it grew by more than 0.15 of the median edge length are spikes the bake added.
8. **Chains and jewellery across the chest** (nipple chains, necklaces, chains hanging between piercings) must keep their links whole and linked. A plain conform stretched the link in the cleavage to about 5 times its length, because the body under it moves much more than at its ends. What didn't work:
   - Masking the middle links: they stay behind while the rest moves, and the chain comes apart there.
   - `rigidify_slider` per link after a plain conform: each link keeps its shape, but neighbouring links take different moves, and gaps of up to 1.4 opened between them.
   - The whole chain as one rigid part (`perPart: false`): it detached from the loops it hangs from (0.67).
   - Loops on the body conversion and the chain on the UniBoob one: they came apart by 2.2. Convert every shape of a piece with the same reference.

   What worked: the UniBoob reference only, for the whole piece, conformed wide and smooth so neighbouring links get nearly the same move: `ConformSliders` with `ProximityRadius: 20, MaxResults: 1000, SmoothResults: true, SmoothIterations: 12, SmoothStrength: 0.5`. Then `rigidify_slider` the conversion slider per part on the link shapes, slider at 1, `set_base_shape`. Mask parts that should follow the body first, such as a necklace's band around the neck (`mask_ops` `parts`). On 10 chain pieces no link deformed and no pair of touching links opened by more than 0.09; the ends stayed in their loops. The same holds for a body's sliders in the target game: conform the UniBoob reference wide, then load the body with `mergeSliders: true, appendNewSliders: true` and conform the sliders UniBoob has no data for (in Skyrim CBBE the nipple and `_v2` sliders, `BreastCenterBig`, `SternumDepth`, `SternumHeight`) from the body the same wide way. Without that, `validate_project` reports them as missing morphs and the chain doesn't follow them. Then rigidify every slider per link. Chains attached to a jacket and converted with its blend opened by up to 0.15.

   **Nipple piercings and nipple chains** came out floating off the nipple after the UniBoob-only conversion, because the Fallout 4 UniBoob mesh has no nipples. Blending in the body conversion at the nipples opened gaps of up to 0.33 between links. What worked: keep the converted chain, and move each whole piece with `transform_shapes`. Use a small `scale` along x about `origin: "zero"`, so both sides move in or out without tearing a chain that crosses the middle, plus a `move`. Measure the offset against the body where the piercing goes through: import the source piece next to the converted one and run `compare_shapes` with `anchorTo` the body and `sourcePreset` the preset the source was made on. Each part's `restoreMove` is the move that gives it back the offset it had to the body points it touches in the source. Don't measure against the breast's front-most vertex, which was 2–3 units from the nipple and gave a move twice too large. The fix was 0.08–0.14 back and 0.08–0.19 down. The wide, smoothed Fallout 4 → Skyrim conversion moved them off again, by 0.16 forward and 0.14 up on all three pieces. The same move fixed that before the sliders.

   Don't give a necklace's band the wide conform: smoothed over a radius of 20, it sank into the neck and chest (233 vertices on a heart necklace). Conform the links wide with the band masked (`mask_ops` `parts`), then invert the mask and conform the band at the default radius, smoothed. That left 109 vertices inside. To clear the rest, mask the links and `fit_to_body` the band with `gapMode: "keep", minGap: 0.05, minNormalAgreement: -1`: 0 inside, and the inner face of the band is pulled too. Do the sliders the same way: links wide, band at the default radius, then `rigidify_slider` with the band masked. The result had no clipping on presets or big breast sliders, and the clasp stayed on the band.

   A straight strap between two cups (a bikini's centre bar) bows forward with either conversion: the cup corners move back more than the cleavage, or the UniBoob fill, under the strap's middle (a bow of 1.0 against 0.15 in the source). Give the strap the UniBoob conversion only and `rigidify_slider` it with `allowScale: true`: it stayed straight (0.19), and its ends stayed 0.17 from the cups (0.08 in the source; 0.3 without scaling, because the cups moved apart). In a blend project, pass `clearance: -1`: the reference there still has the source preset's bigger breasts, and the default clearance pushed the strap 2.8 off the cups. `analyze_strap` measures the bow and how far each end is from the cups, also per preset (`presets`, `cases`). The best rigid fit still left the ends visibly off the cup corners. To join them, import the source next to the bake and `compare_shapes` the strap with its source copy, `anchorTo` the cups and `sourceAnchorTo` the source's cups: `restoreMove` is where the ends belong. Then `transform_shapes` the strap with a per-axis `scale` along its length about `origin: "center"`, plus a `move`: here x 0.923 and a move of 0.38 back and 0.39 up put both ends within 0.02 of their place.

   For the target body's sliders, `rigidify_slider` is the wrong tool for such a strap. The cups spread or close with the sliders, and a rigid strap came off them by up to 0.38. A rigid middle with masked ends stayed on, but kinked by up to 0.72. What worked: conform the strap like the cups (UniBoob, plus the body for the sliders UniBoob has no data for). Mask only the end loops around the cup corners (`mask_ops` `box`). Then `smooth_slider_deltas` on the strap without `slider`, which smooths every slider with data on it, with `iterations: 100, strength: 1` (the most it takes). The masked ends keep the cups' moves, and the smoothing spreads them evenly along the strap. On ten presets and sliders it stayed as straight as at rest (0.11–0.16 off a straight line), with its ends on the cup corners. BreastsTogether, which shortens it, flips the thin strap's triangles without showing.

   To check, export the source and the bake to OBJ and measure per pair of touching parts how far apart they are after the bake (closest vertex distance minus the source's), and per part how much its edges stretch.
9. **Folds under and between the breasts:** after a big preset change, cloth spanning the underbust keeps small folds and dents from the old shape. All pieces sit on one body after the bake, so one box fits them all: on CBBE x −7.5 to 7.5, y above 5, z −33 to −25. Per piece, as steps: `ClearMask`, `MaskBox` and `InvertMask` on the cloth shapes, `SmoothMask` 3, `SmoothVertices` 30 at 0.2 with the trim as `FollowShapes`, `ClearMask`, `ExportFile`. On 48 tops this lowered the roughness there by 10–40% and left clipping at rest the same on 46 (a one-piece went from 229 to 261 vertices inside the body, a tube top from 116 to 123). Trim stayed on its cloth: in 41 of 48 no trim vertex moved more than 0.1 off it, the worst was a jacket zipper at 0.27.
10. **Push-up pieces on the base body:** a top modelled on a pushed-up preset sits higher than the base body's breasts, and the lower breast shows below the cup rim. That's the shape difference, not a conform error; bring the hem down where it shows.

Head items (masks, glasses, face piercings) aren't affected by a body preset and skip this.

## Converting a whole mod

- **List the pieces first:** compare the NIFs (vertex and triangle counts, positions, UVs) to find exact duplicates and texture-only variants. Texture swaps don't need their own set; the plugin points to other textures.
- Use the source game's instance for the preset step and the target game's for the rest, and batch both with scripts (see `basics`, "Many outfits at once"). One script per kind of piece covers most of a mod: worn with the body, without the body, feet, hands.
- **Pieces with feet or hands:** Fallout 4 shoes and gloves often include part of the body's feet or hands; leave those out with `skipShapes` and load "CBBE Feet" or "CBBE Hands" as the target, saved with them. Make `FeetZap` or `HandsZap` from what they cover (`conform-sliders`, "Zap sliders"), with `inside` around 1.2 so the zap also hides where the foot pokes through the shoe. Render the shoe alone before deciding a boot is open: feet poking through a closed toe box looked like an open-toe boot. The body pieces a Fallout 4 shoe brings along aren't always feet (`CBBE_outfit` in a heel set was the torso); check with `list_shapes` bounds.
- **High heels** are modelled around a foot on tiptoe, which "CBBE Feet" isn't: conformed and weighted to the flat foot, the shoe follows the wrong surface. Load feet posed for heels instead when `list_reference_templates` has them (e.g. "CBBE Feet High Heels", posed for heels about 8 units high), or make them once from a converted pair of heels: its feet without the shoes and outfit-only sliders, saved as a set and added to a `RefTemplates` file. Heels need the `HH_OFFSET` NiFloatExtraData (`set_extra_data`) on the shoe shapes, as other Skyrim heels have it. A closed boot that covers the whole foot can be saved without the feet (`copyReference: false`); slot 37 still hides the game's feet.
- In `run_steps`, the `CopyBoneWeights` step leaves vertices whose copied weights add up to less than 1 (a bodysuit had 230, up to 0.11 short); give it `Normalize: true`. The `copy_bone_weights` tool cleans and normalizes on its own.
- Pieces without a body (belts, pants, jewellery) still conform to and copy weights from the body; save them with `copyReference: false`.
- **Cloth over the nipples:** conformed `NipBGone` dents cups and tops where the nipple flattens. `clear_slider_data` it on the cloth shapes only, so the body keeps it. Keep the other nipple sliders and don't add zaps when the mod has see-through retextures of the piece.
- **Check by looking, from more than one side:** a contact sheet per piece helped catch what the numbers missed: source game, target base and a target preset, front and three-quarter side. Close-ups from above show how cloth and straps span the cleavage.
- **Redoing a piece** after its saved set got shaders, gloss, environment maps or transparency set up: copy the set's shape data NIF aside before rebuilding the set from the source, then `copy_shader` with `file` = that copy puts every shape's shader, textures and alpha back exactly as they were.
- **Head items** (masks, glasses, headbands, face piercings) need the new game's head, not only the head bone's offset: Skyrim's face sits 0.5–1 further forward than Fallout 4's, so masks and headbands sank into it. Make a head conversion once:
  - Import both games' female heads (`load_from_archive` in the source game's instance, `shape_transforms` `apply` so they're in global space, `export_file`, then `import_from_game`), with the target game's head as reference.
  - `fit_to_body` the old head onto it with `gap: 0`, `minGap: 0`, `relief: 0`, `asSlider: "HeadConv"`. Fallout 4's head mesh is only the face, so leave `symmetric` off, or the face is stretched over the back of the head.
  - Make the old head the reference and save it as a set.
  - Per item: conform `HeadConv` (`ProximityRadius` 15, smoothed), `rigidify_slider` piercings and glasses (`perPart: false`, `clearance: -1`), slider at 1, `set_base_shape`.
  - Then load the target head as reference, `CopyBoneWeights` with `BoneList: ["NPC Head [Head]"]`, and `fit_to_body` `gapMode: "keep"` on cloth items (masks, headbands) to push out what still clips.
  - A mask and a headband came out with 0 and 3 vertices inside the head. Lip and nose rings sat on the lips and nostrils.

## Partitions and segments

- **Skyrim and older** split skinned shapes into partitions, each with a body slot that decides what hides it when another item with that slot is worn: 30 head, 31 hair, 32 body, 33 hands, 34 forearms, 35 amulet, 36 ring, 37 feet, 38 calves, 39 shield, 40 tail, 41 long hair, 42 circlet, 43 ears, and 44–61 free slots mods use. `get_partitions`, `set_partitions` (assign masked triangles to a slot), `copy_partitions` from the reference.
- `copy_partitions` from the body gives everything slot 32, and 34 or 38 where a piece lies over the forearms or calves (jeans got 38 on the legs). Pieces worn together with a body piece need another slot (`ChangePartitions` step, `"32"` → `"52"`, and the same for `"34"` and `"38"`). What mods commonly use: 44 face or mouth, 45 neck, 46 chest (harness), 47 back, 48 misc, 49 pelvis (panties, pants), 52 pelvis secondary (skirts, belts), 53 and 54 legs, 55 face jewellery, 56 chest secondary, 57 shoulder, 58 and 59 arms, 60 and 61 misc. Follow what the mod's earlier conversions used when there are any.
- **Fallout 4** uses segments and subsegments with a segment file (`.ssf`): `set_segments`, `copy_partitions`.
- Every triangle needs a partition: `validate_project` lists those without.

## Shaders and materials

### Skyrim (LE and SE)

The shader (`BSLightingShaderProperty`) is in the NIF. `get_shape_properties` shows it; `set_shader_properties` changes the shader type and values, `set_texture_paths` the textures, `set_geometry_properties` the geometry flags.

- **Texture slots:** 0 diffuse (`_d`; alpha is transparency when the shape has an alpha property), 1 normal map (`_n`; **alpha is the specular mask**), 2 glow (`_g`, glow map shader) or subsurface (`_sk`, skin tint shader), 3 height for parallax (`_p`), 4 environment cube map (`_e`), 5 environment mask (`_m`), 6 subsurface or inner layer, 7 back light (`_b`) or specular. Paths are relative to Data, e.g. `textures\armor\outfit\outfit_d.dds`.
- **Specular:** the normal map's alpha masks it, `SpecularPower` (glossiness) sets how sharp the highlight is for the whole shape, `SpecularStrength` and `SpecularColor` its brightness and color.
- **Other features:** two-sided, rim, back and soft lighting, glow (`EmissiveColor`, `EmissiveMultiple`), alpha blending and testing (an alpha property, `set_alpha_property`).
- **Environment mapping** needs a cube map in slot 4 and a mask in slot 5. Complex Material mods read an extended mask from slot 5, with a dynamic cube map in slot 4.
- **Bodies** use model-space normal maps (`_msn`); outfits use tangent-space ones.
- **True PBR** (Community Shaders) sets a shader flag and an RMAOS map in slot 5. PBR mods usually patch the built meshes after BodySlide, so a BodySlide source mesh of a PBR mod isn't PBR; don't "fix" that.
- **Texture formats:** Skyrim SE takes BC7; Skyrim LE doesn't (use BC1 without alpha, BC3 with alpha).
- Transparent effects (glass, energy) use an effect shader (`BSEffectShaderProperty`), not the lighting shader.

### Fallout 4

The NIF's shader names a material file (BGSM, or BGEM for effects) under `Materials\`, which the game reads instead of the NIF's values. `read_material` shows one; `write_material` writes one (below).

- **BGSM textures:** diffuse (`_d`), normal (`_n`, usually BC5 with only red and green; the game rebuilds blue), smoothness/specular (`_s`: **red is specular, green is smoothness**), plus glow, environment, greyscale (palette color), inner layer, wrinkles and displacement maps.
- Specular, smoothness, two-sided, alpha, rim and back lighting, subsurface and glow are material values.

### Fallout 4 to Skyrim

`import_from_game` with `dataFolders` sets textures, specular, glossiness, two-sided, alpha, glow, rim and back lighting from the BGSM. What it can't do, and its `notes` say:

- **Normal map with specular:** build a new normal map from the Fallout 4 `_n` with blue rebuilt (z = √(1 − x² − y²)) and the `_s` red channel as alpha. Save it as BC7 (SE) or BC3 (LE). Red and green point the same way in both games (red along +U, green along +V, i.e. down the image: DirectX style), so keep them as they are and don't flip green.
- **SpecularPower:** the tool guesses it from the material's smoothness alone, which is too high when the `_s` green (gloss) is dark. Set it from the average of the `_s` green: about 2^(gloss·10+1). A wetsuit came out at 28, its belt at 24.
- **Environment mapping** isn't set up: make a mask for slot 5 and pick a cube map for slot 4.
- **Greyscale-to-palette coloring** has no Skyrim equivalent: bake the color into the diffuse.
- **BGEM effect materials** need an effect shader in Skyrim; only their textures are set.
- **PBR materials** (Fallout 76) don't map to Skyrim's lighting shader.

`convert_textures` makes Skyrim and Fallout 4 maps from PBR maps (base color, normal, roughness or gloss, metallic, ambient occlusion, opacity); see `external-models`. For channel work it doesn't cover, like rebuilding a Fallout 4 normal map's blue channel, tell the user which maps to make and how, or use a texture tool (e.g. texconv) if one is available to you.

### Skyrim to Fallout 4

The shapes need a BGSM per material (a BGEM for effect shaders). **`write_material`** writes them:

- `shape` starts from a Skyrim shape's shader: textures, specular, smoothness from `SpecularPower`, two-sided, alpha, glow, rim, back and soft lighting. Its `notes` say what's missing.
- `base` starts from another material file instead, e.g. a vanilla one with the right look; `properties` changes values on top, named as `read_material` returns them (`"textures": {"smoothSpec": "MyOutfit/suit_s.dds"}`).
- Write into the mod's `Materials` folder, e.g. `D:/Mods/MyOutfit/Materials/MyOutfit/suit.bgsm`; texture paths are relative to `Textures`. Fallout 4 is version 2 (the default), Fallout 76 20 to 22.
- In a Fallout 4 project, `assignToShapes` names the material in the shapes' shaders. The window shows it once the file is in the game's Data folder.

Textures: the diffuse stays; the Skyrim normal map's alpha becomes the red of an `_s` map (with smoothness in green), and the normal map loses its alpha. Without an `_s` map the shape has no specular, so make one and set `smoothSpec`.

A Fallout 4 shape's own material can be changed the same way: `write_material` with `shape` starts from the material file it has loaded.

### Starfield

Starfield keeps geometry in separate `.mesh` files, morphs in `morph.dat` files (`export_slider_data` `dat`) and materials in its material database. The tools don't map Starfield materials, and skinned vertices can have up to 8 bones. Convert the shape and sliders as above, and tell the user what the materials need.
